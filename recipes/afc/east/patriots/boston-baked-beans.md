## Ingredients

- 2 pounds dried white or navy beans
- ½ cup unsulfured molasses, plus a little more to adjust at the end if desired
- 1 tablespoon plus 1 teaspoon dry mustard
- ½ cup packed dark brown sugar
- 1 large onion, peeled and diced
- 12 ounces salt pork, or bacon, cut into two pieces
- 5 cups water, plus more hot water as needed during baking
- ½ teaspoon freshly ground black pepper, plus more to taste
- 1 tablespoon plus 1 teaspoon salt, added at the end and adjusted to taste

### Optional

- A splash of cider vinegar to finish

## Instructions

1. The night before, pick through the beans and discard debris or shriveled beans. Cover with at least 2–3 inches of cold water and soak 8–12 hours. Drain and rinse.
2. Heat oven to 300°F. In a saucepan, whisk together molasses, mustard, brown sugar, diced onion, pepper and 5 cups water. Bring to a boil, stirring until the sugar dissolves.
3. Score the salt pork about ¼ inch deep at 1-inch intervals (or use bacon pieces). Put the meat and drained beans in a covered bean pot or deep, oven-safe casserole. Pour over the molasses mixture; the liquid should cover the beans by about ½ inch. Add hot water if needed.
4. Cover and bake about 5 hours, checking every 45 minutes. Add hot water as needed to keep the beans covered. Uncover for the final 50–60 minutes and continue baking until beans are tender and the liquid has thickened to a glaze; total oven time is about 6 hours.
5. Remove from the oven. Stir in salt to taste, then adjust pepper and molasses if needed. Add a small splash of cider vinegar if desired. Rest 10 minutes before serving; the sauce thickens as it cools.

## Kitchen Notes

Timing note: Slow covered baking of soaked dried navy beans with molasses and salt pork restores the traditional long-cook profile. Smithsonian advises holding salt until the end; its accompanying claim that early salt prevents tenderness is not repeated here as settled fact. Check tenderness and liquid during the long bake; bean age and pot shape affect timing.
