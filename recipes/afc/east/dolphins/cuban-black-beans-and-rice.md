## Ingredients

### Beans

- 1 green bell pepper, roughly chopped
- 1 medium yellow onion, roughly chopped
- 4 garlic cloves
- 1/4 cup olive oil
- 1 teaspoon dried oregano
- Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
- 1 pound dried black beans
- 1/4 cup distilled white vinegar
- 1/4 cup vino seco or sherry cooking wine
- 2 bay leaves
- 2 tablespoons sugar
- 5 cups water

### Rice

- 2 cups long-grain white rice
- 3 cups water
- 1/2 teaspoon salt

## Instructions

1. Rinse rice in a sieve until water runs mostly clear. Combine rice, 3 cups water and 1/2 teaspoon salt in a saucepan. Bring to a boil, cover, reduce heat to low and cook until water is absorbed and rice is tender, about 15 to 18 minutes. Remove from heat and rest covered 5 minutes; fluff.
2. Finely chop bell pepper, onion and garlic in a food processor or by hand. Set a 6- to 8-quart Instant Pot to saute and add olive oil. Add vegetables, oregano, 1 tablespoon salt and black pepper; cook, stirring occasionally, until vegetables soften and onion turns translucent, about 5 minutes.
3. Add beans, vinegar, vino seco, bay leaves, sugar and 5 cups water. Lock lid and pressure cook on high for 30 minutes; allow about 10 minutes for pressure to build.
4. Let pressure release naturally for 20 minutes, then carefully release any remaining steam and remove lid. Discard bay leaves. Remove 1 cup beans, pulse almost smooth in a food processor or mash in the pot, then return and stir.
5. Set cooker to saute and simmer until beans thicken, about 10 minutes. Serve beans over the cooked rice.

## Kitchen Notes

Timing note: Food Network lists 15 minutes active, 1 hour 30 minutes total and 6 to 8 bean servings, and calls rice optional. The booklet adds an explicit rice quantity and stovetop method because rice is required for this dish. Rice quantities and its 20 to 23 minute cook/rest estimate are editorial additions; the bean cook follows the cited pressure-cooker source. Cook the rice concurrently with pressure cooking to keep the total elapsed time near the bean source's 90 minutes.
