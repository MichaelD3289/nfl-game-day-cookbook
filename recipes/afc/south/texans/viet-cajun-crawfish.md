## Ingredients

- 15 pounds live crawfish
- 1/2 to 1 1/2 cups powdered crawfish boil spice, plus more to taste for the second batch
- 1/2 cup unfiltered apple cider vinegar
- 1/2 bunch celery, halved
- 3 stalks lemongrass, coarsely chopped
- 3 heads garlic, halved
- 1/2 large knob each ginger and galangal, chopped
- 1/2 bunch Thai basil
- 2 citrus (makrut lime) leaves
- 3 jalapeños, halved
- 4 oranges, 1 lemon and 3 limes, halved
- 1 pound small new potatoes
- 1/2 pound carrots
- 12 boiling onions
- 1/2 pound long beans
- 1 pound snow or sugar snap peas
- 1/2 pound shiitake or oyster mushrooms

### Garlic butter sauce

- 1/2 pound (2 sticks) butter
- 4–6 cloves garlic, minced
- Crawfish boil spice, to taste
- 3 limes, cut into wedges

## Instructions

1. Rinse the crawfish in several changes of cold water until it runs clear; discard any dead ones (standard practice, not in the source).
2. Fill a 30-quart pot with a strainer basket two-thirds full of water. Add 1/2 cup boil spice, the vinegar, celery, lemongrass, garlic, ginger, galangal, basil, citrus leaves and jalapeños, and squeeze in the citrus. The source lists lemongrass, galangal and citrus leaves but its method omits them; this book adds them with the other aromatics. Bring to a boil.
3. Add the potatoes, carrots and onions; cover and simmer briskly 10 minutes. Add the long beans, peas and mushrooms; cover and cook 2–3 minutes. Lift out the vegetables and keep warm.
4. Meanwhile, melt 1/4 cup of the butter and cook the garlic over medium heat until fragrant and translucent. Add the remaining butter and season with boil spice.
5. Add half the crawfish. When the broth bubbles, turn off the heat, cover and steep about 10 minutes, until the crawfish sink. Drain into a large bowl and gently toss with half the vegetables, half the sauce and some lime wedges.
6. Return the broth to a boil, add more spice to taste and repeat with the rest of the crawfish, vegetables, sauce and limes.

## Kitchen Notes

Viet-Cajun crawfish grew out of Houston's Vietnamese and Louisiana communities. Lemongrass, ginger and citrus are common Houston additions to the Cajun boil, and a garlic-butter toss after cooking is the style's signature. The source cooks its 30 pounds in three 10-pound batches; this half batch uses two.
