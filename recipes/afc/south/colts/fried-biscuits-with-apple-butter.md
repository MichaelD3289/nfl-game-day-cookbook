## Ingredients

### Biga

- 1 cup warm (100°F to 110°F) spring or filtered water
- 1 teaspoon instant yeast
- 1 cup all-purpose flour
- 1 teaspoon granulated sugar

### Biscuit dough

- 4 cups all-purpose flour, plus more for flouring the board
- 2 tablespoons granulated sugar
- 2 teaspoons salt
- ¼ cup lard or butter, well chilled and cut into small bits
- ¼ cup nonfat buttermilk
- The reserved biga

### To fry and serve

- 1 to 2 cups lard or vegetable oil, for frying {{no-scale}}
- Apple butter or preserves, for serving

## Instructions

1. Combine the biga ingredients in a bowl with room to triple, cover tightly, and let rise in a warm spot for 24 to 36 hours.
2. Sift the flour, sugar and salt. Rub in the cold lard or butter until the mixture looks like fine meal. Stir in the buttermilk, let rest 1 minute, then gently mix in the biga.
3. Knead briefly, just until the dough forms a ball, then let rise in a covered bowl about 1 hour, until doubled.
4. Roll the dough to ½ inch thick, cut 2-inch biscuits, and let them rise for another 20 minutes.
5. Heat about ½ inch of lard or oil in a 10-inch cast-iron skillet to 350°F. Fry the biscuits in batches, leaving at least ½ inch between them, 3 to 4 minutes on the first side and about 3 minutes on the second, until golden brown, keeping the fat at roughly ¼ inch. Open one to check doneness.
6. Drain on paper towels and serve warm with apple butter or preserves.

## Kitchen Notes

Bounded by Buns, which published this recipe from Fehribach's Midwestern Food, says it is based on the Nashville House restaurant in Nashville, Indiana, in Brown County, and quotes the book calling the biscuits a cross between biscuits and a fried doughnut.
