## Ingredients

### Dough

- 3 cups unbleached all-purpose flour
- 1 tablespoon baking powder
- 1 tablespoon sugar
- 1 teaspoon kosher salt
- Pinch of freshly grated nutmeg
- 1 cup vegetable shortening
- 1/2 cup water
- 4 tablespoons (1/2 stick) unsalted butter, melted, for brushing

### Filling

- 8 ounces sharp cheddar cheese, shredded (about 2 cups)
- 2 tablespoons unbleached all-purpose flour
- 4 large eggs, lightly beaten
- 1/2 teaspoon kosher salt
- 1/8 teaspoon datil pepper hot sauce
- Pinch of freshly grated nutmeg

## Instructions

1. Place the oven racks in the bottom and top positions and heat the oven to 425°F. Line two baking sheets with parchment paper.
2. Dough: whisk the flour, baking powder, sugar, salt and nutmeg in a large bowl. Cut in the shortening with two knives or a dough blender. Add the water and stir until the dough comes together into a ball. Cover and set aside.
3. Filling: toss the cheese with the flour in a medium bowl. Stir in the eggs, salt, hot sauce and nutmeg.
4. Roll the dough 1/8-inch thick on a lightly floured surface and cut 24 circles with a 3-inch biscuit cutter. Put a well-rounded teaspoon of filling on one side of each, fold over into a half-moon and pinch the edges to seal. Re-roll the scraps for any remaining filling.
5. Brush the pastries with the butter and cut two 1-inch slashes in the tops to make a cross. Place on the baking sheets.
6. Bake one sheet at a time on the bottom rack about 10 minutes, until lightly browned and the cheese puffs through the crosses. Move to the top rack and bake 2 to 3 minutes more, until well browned and crisp. Repeat with the second sheet. Serve warm or at room temperature.

## Kitchen Notes

The source calls these fried cheese tarts, though its method bakes them. They were traditionally handed out to singers serenading St. Augustine's old neighborhoods on a spring night each year, and the Fromajardis Serenade still continues the week after Easter. The source gives a separate datil pepper hot sauce recipe (makes about 2 cups) for the small amount used here.
