## Ingredients

- 1 packet (about 115 g, or 4 oz) kasseri cheese
- 1 tablespoon water, plus extra if needed
- 1/4 cup plain flour, plus extra if needed
- 1 tablespoon olive oil
- 2 tablespoons brandy, at room temperature
- Half a lemon, or more to taste
- 1 tablespoon chopped fresh flat-leaf parsley

## Instructions

1. Measure everything out before the pan goes on the stove. Brush both sides of the cheese with the water, then press each side into the flour until fully coated.
2. Heat a well-seasoned cast iron pan over medium-high heat until it just starts to smoke, then add the olive oil.
3. Lay the cheese in the hot oil and fry for about 2 minutes, until a golden crust forms and the cheese begins to ooze. Turn it quickly with a spatula and fry the second side for about 30 seconds.
4. Take the pan off the heat and set it on a heatproof board or plate.
5. Pour the brandy over the cheese and light it with a long fireplace lighter. Squeeze the lemon over the top to put out the last flames, then scatter over the parsley.

## Kitchen Notes

Serve with sliced fresh or grilled bread. Chicago Magazine's 2016 piece "Opa! In Praise of Flaming Saganaki" says Chris Liakouras, who opened the Parthenon in Greektown in 1968, took credit for the flaming and the "Opa!", and that the Parthenon dipped its kasseri in flour and egg wash; this version skips the egg wash.
