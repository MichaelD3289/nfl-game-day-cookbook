## Ingredients

- 2 tablespoons plus 1/2 cup high-temperature cooking oil, such as grapeseed oil
- 1 pound boneless chicken thighs
- 1 pound smoked andouille sausage
- 2 ribs celery, medium diced
- 1 large bell pepper, medium diced
- 1 large onion, medium diced
- 8 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 teaspoons kosher salt
- 2 teaspoons black pepper
- 1 teaspoon cayenne pepper
- 1/2 cup all-purpose flour
- 1 cup amber lager or amber ale (for example Abita Amber); this beer-roux version is a New Orleans tourism recipe, not universal jambalaya technique
- 6 cups low-sodium chicken stock
- 2 cups uncooked long-grain white rice
- 3 tablespoons room-temperature unsalted butter
- 1/4 cup thinly sliced green onions

## Instructions

1. Heat oven to 350°F. Season chicken thighs with the source’s 2 teaspoons salt, 2 teaspoons black pepper and 1 teaspoon cayenne. Slice andouille into bite-size rounds.
2. In a Dutch oven, heat 2 tablespoons oil and sear chicken about 5 minutes per side; remove. Brown sliced andouille in the same pot for about 3–5 minutes; remove with chicken.
3. Add remaining 1/2 cup oil, then whisk in 1/2 cup flour. Stir continuously over medium heat until the roux reaches a dark-chocolate color without black flecks. Immediately add diced celery, bell pepper, onion and minced garlic; cook 1 minute to stop the roux darkening.
4. Stir in 1 cup amber lager or amber ale (for example Abita Amber); this beer-roux version is a New Orleans tourism recipe, not universal jambalaya technique for 30 seconds, then slowly add 6 cups low-sodium chicken stock while stirring. Return chicken and andouille, bring to a simmer, cover and cook 1 hour.
5. Stir in 2 cups uncooked long-grain white rice, cover and bake 30 minutes. Check that rice is tender and chicken reaches 165°F; if rice is firm, add a little hot stock and continue covered in 5-minute increments.
6. Stir in 3 tablespoons butter, sprinkle with sliced green onions and serve.

## Kitchen Notes

Timing note: Source header lists 1 hour cook but method includes another 30 minutes after adding rice.
