## Ingredients

### Syrup

- 1 piloncillo cone (or 1/2 cup packed dark brown sugar plus 1 tablespoon molasses)
- 1/2 cup water

### Dough

- 500 grams all-purpose flour
- 250 grams lard
- 1 1/2 teaspoons active dry yeast
- 1/4 cup water
- 1/2 teaspoon kosher salt
- Additional flour, as needed

### Filling

- 2 piloncillo cones

## Instructions

1. Simmer 1 piloncillo cone with 1/2 cup water to make a syrup. Cool to room temperature, no warmer than 110°F. In a bowl, mix the flour and yeast; cut in the lard. Slowly knead in the cooled syrup, then add room-temperature water a tablespoon at a time (about 3 tablespoons, up to 1/4 cup), until the dough is slightly tacky but not sticky. Mix in the salt and knead 2 minutes more.
2. Knead the dough into a smooth ball. Put it in a lightly greased bowl, turn to coat, cover with a damp towel, and rest 1 hour.
3. Grate the 2 piloncillo cones for the filling and toss with a little flour. Divide the dough into an even number of golf-ball-size pieces. Roll each into a thin 5-inch disk.
4. Put about 1 tablespoon filling on half the disks, leaving a 1/2-inch border. Dampen the edges, top with the remaining disks, seal, and poke vent holes. Rest 15 minutes.
5. Bake at 350°F for 15 to 18 minutes, until slightly soft to the touch. Cool before serving.

## Kitchen Notes

KJZZ's recipe is by Minerva Orduño Rincon, whom the station's accompanying report describes making coyotas at home in Tempe; the report traces the pastry to Hermosillo, Sonora.
