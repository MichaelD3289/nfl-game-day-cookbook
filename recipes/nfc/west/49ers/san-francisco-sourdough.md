## Ingredients

### Wild yeast starter

- 1/4 cup (2 ounces / 56.5 grams) mother starter, cold or at room temperature
- 1 3/4 cups (8 ounces / 227 grams) unbleached bread flour
- 1/2 cup plus 2 tablespoons (5 ounces / 142 grams) water, at room temperature

### Dough

- All of the wild yeast starter (15 ounces / 425 grams)
- 1 3/4 cups (14 ounces / 397 grams) lukewarm water (about 95°F or 35°C)
- 4 1/2 cups (20 ounces / 567 grams) unbleached bread flour
- 2 1/2 teaspoons (0.63 ounce / 18 grams) salt, or 3 1/2 teaspoons coarse kosher salt
- 2 1/4 teaspoons (0.25 ounce / 7 grams) instant yeast (optional)
- 1 cup hot water, for the oven's steam pan

## Instructions

1. Mix the starter ingredients until blended. Knead about 30 seconds, place in a lightly oiled bowl, cover loosely, and rest at room temperature 6 to 8 hours until 1 1/2 times its size. If using it the same day, let it ferment 1 more hour; otherwise refrigerate up to 3 days.
2. Cut the starter into 10 to 12 pieces and mix with the lukewarm water about 1 minute to soften. Add the flour, salt, and optional yeast. Mix 2 minutes, rest 5 minutes, then mix 4 minutes more until the dough is soft, supple, and slightly sticky. Knead 1 minute, rest uncovered 10 minutes, stretch and fold, rest covered 10 minutes, then stretch and fold again and shape into a ball.
3. For the purist version without yeast, let the dough sit at room temperature 1 1/2 to 2 hours before refrigerating. With yeast, refrigerate immediately. The dough is ready the next day and can be used for up to 3 days.
4. For the purist version, remove dough from the refrigerator 4 hours before baking and shape after 2 hours. For the yeast version, remove it 2 hours before baking and shape right away. In either case, proof the shaped loaf for 2 hours, until 1 1/2 times its size and springy.
5. About 20 minutes before baking, heat the oven to 500°F with an empty steam pan. Score the loaf, transfer to the oven, pour 1 cup hot water into the steam pan, and lower the oven to 450°F. Bake 12 minutes, rotate, then bake 15 to 35 minutes more, depending on loaf size. Cool on a wire rack at least 1 hour before serving.

## Kitchen Notes

Peter Reinhart calls this San Francisco sourdough for its all-white-flour style, while noting the Bay Area's microorganisms are needed for the bread to be true San Francisco sourdough.
