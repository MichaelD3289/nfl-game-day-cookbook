## Ingredients

- 1 cup extra-virgin olive oil, divided
- 16 tablespoons unsalted butter, divided
- 1 medium yellow onion, finely chopped
- 2 medium carrots, finely chopped
- 1 green bell pepper, finely chopped
- 1 celery stalk, finely chopped
- 1 medium leek, white part only, cleaned and chopped
- 1/2 small fennel bulb, finely chopped
- 2 (28-ounce) cans crushed Italian tomatoes
- 2 tablespoons tomato paste
- 4 cups water
- 4 bay leaves
- 1 teaspoon each dried basil, oregano and thyme
- 2 pinches cayenne pepper
- 1–2 cups all-purpose flour, for dredging
- 1 1/2 pounds halibut or other firm white fish, large pieces
- 16 sea scallops
- 16 large raw shrimp, peeled and deveined
- 1/2 pound raw bay shrimp, if available (or very small shrimp)
- 2 garlic cloves, finely chopped
- 12 ounces crabmeat, preferably Dungeness
- 2 cups dry white wine
- 16 live Manila clams, scrubbed
- 1/2 bunch parsley, chopped
- Kosher salt and black pepper, to taste
- Toasted sourdough bread, optional

## Instructions

1. In a large soup pot over medium heat, melt half the butter with 1/2 cup olive oil. Soften onion 2 minutes; add carrots, bell pepper, celery, leek and fennel and cook about 5 minutes until onion turns translucent.
2. Add crushed tomatoes, tomato paste, 4 cups water, bay leaves, basil, oregano, thyme, cayenne, salt and pepper. Bring to a boil, then simmer gently 2 hours, stirring occasionally.
3. Dredge halibut, sea scallops and both shrimp sizes lightly in flour; shake off excess. Heat remaining 1/2 cup oil and remaining butter in a wide skillet over high heat. Add garlic for 1–2 minutes, then briefly brown seafood in batches, 1–2 minutes per side. Transfer to soup pot.
4. Add Dungeness crabmeat, cover and simmer gently 10–15 minutes. In the emptied skillet, add white wine and scrape up browned bits; add clams, cover and cook about 5 minutes until they open. Discard any clams that remain closed.
5. Pour clams and skillet broth into the stew; taste and adjust salt and pepper. Remove bay leaves, top with parsley and serve with toasted sourdough.

## Kitchen Notes

Dungeness crab is the local choice; use the freshest seafood available.
