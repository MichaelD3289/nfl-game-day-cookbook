## Ingredients

- 4 pounds beef chuck, trimmed and cut into 1-inch cubes
- 3 whole dried New Mexico or California chile peppers
- 3 whole dried guajillo chile peppers
- 3 whole dried ancho chile peppers
- 6 cups low-sodium beef stock (traditional beef-forward choice); chicken or vegetable stock also works
- 2 tablespoons vegetable oil
- 1 large onion, chopped
- 1 large fresh poblano pepper, seeded and chopped
- 6 medium garlic cloves (2 tablespoons), minced
- 1 cinnamon stick, 3 inches long
- 1 tablespoon ground cumin
- 1/2 teaspoon ground allspice
- 1/4 to 1 teaspoon cayenne pepper or chipotle powder, optional for added heat
- 1 tablespoon masa harina, or 1–2 small corn tortillas torn into pieces
- Salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste

## Instructions

1. Remove stems and seeds from dried chiles, cut them into pieces and toast in a dry skillet over medium heat about 5 minutes until fragrant.
2. Cover toasted chiles with hot water and let soften for 20 minutes. Transfer to a blender with 2 cups stock and blend until smooth.
3. Heat about 1 tablespoon oil in a Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Brown half the beef on two sides, transfer to a bowl, and repeat with the rest, adding oil as needed.
4. If the pot is dry, add about 1 teaspoon or more oil. Cook onion and poblano about 5 minutes until soft. Add garlic, cinnamon, cumin, allspice and optional cayenne; cook 1 minute.
5. Return beef to the pot. Add chile purée and remaining 4 cups stock, scraping up browned bits. Bring to a boil, reduce to a very low simmer, partially cover and cook 2 1/2–3 hours until fork-tender. Add up to 1 cup more stock or water if it becomes too dry.
6. Mix masa harina with 3 tablespoons water to make a smooth paste and stir it in; alternatively stir in torn tortilla pieces until they disintegrate. After about a minute, remove from heat and season to taste.
7. Remove the cinnamon stick before serving. Taste for salt and heat only after the broth has reduced; add a little water if the chile paste thickens too much.
