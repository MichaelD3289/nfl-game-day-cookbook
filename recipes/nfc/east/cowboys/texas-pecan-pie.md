## Ingredients

### Crust

- 1 3/4 cups all-purpose flour
- 2 tablespoons granulated sugar
- 3/4 teaspoon salt
- 6 tablespoons (3/4 stick) cold unsalted butter, cut into small pieces
- 1/4 cup cold shortening, cut into small pieces
- 7 to 8 tablespoons ice water

### Filling

- 1 1/2 cups packed light brown sugar
- 5 eggs, lightly beaten
- 1/4 cup (1/2 stick) unsalted butter, melted
- 2 1/2 tablespoons all-purpose flour
- 1 1/3 cups light corn syrup
- 1/4 cup dark molasses
- 1 1/2 teaspoons vanilla extract
- 2 1/2 cups pecan pieces
- Vanilla ice cream, for serving

## Instructions

1. Sift the flour, granulated sugar and salt into a large bowl. Rub in the butter and shortening by hand until pea-size, add the ice water and blend until a dough forms. Pat into a ball, cover and refrigerate at least 1 hour.
2. Preheat the oven to 350 degrees F. Roll the dough out to an 11-inch circle on a lightly floured surface, fit it into a 10-inch pie pan and flute the edges. Refrigerate until needed.
3. Whisk together the brown sugar, eggs, butter and flour. Add the corn syrup, molasses and vanilla and whisk until smooth.
4. Put the pecan pieces in the piecrust and pour the filling over them (they will rise to the top while baking).
5. Bake 50 to 60 minutes, until the filling is completely set in the center. Cool on a rack about 1 hour, then slice and serve with vanilla ice cream.

## Kitchen Notes

Denise Gee, a cookbook author and Winnetka Heights resident, submitted this Threadgill's pie to Oak Cliff People from her book Sweet on Texas, calling it the first dessert that sprang to mind as truly Texan. Threadgill's owner Eddie Wilson says that if at least one person in ten doesn't think the pie is scorched, it is not done enough to set the pecans and brown sugar.
