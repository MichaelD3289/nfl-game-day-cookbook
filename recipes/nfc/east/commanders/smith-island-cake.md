## Ingredients

### Cake

- 2 cups sugar
- 2 sticks unsalted butter, cut into chunks
- 5 eggs
- 3 cups flour
- 1/4 teaspoon salt
- heaping teaspoon baking powder
- 1 cup evaporated milk
- 2 teaspoons vanilla
- 1/2 cup water

### Frosting

- 2 sticks butter
- 2 12-oz. cans evaporated milk
- 8 heaping tablespoons unsweetened cocoa
- 2 lbs. confectioners sugar

## Instructions

1. Start the frosting when the first layer goes in the oven. Melt the butter, then stir in the evaporated milk off the heat. Whisk in the cocoa until smooth, return to the heat and cook about 10 minutes without boiling or scorching. Remove from the heat and whisk in the confectioners sugar slowly. Cook slowly until thickened enough to stick to the back of a spoon (it will form a ribbon when drizzled). About 45 minutes in all.
2. Cream the sugar and butter. Add the eggs one at a time, beating until smooth. Sift together the flour, salt and baking powder and mix into the egg mixture one cup at a time. With the mixer running, slowly pour in the evaporated milk, then the vanilla and water, and mix just until uniform.
3. Put three serving spoonfuls of batter in each of 10 lightly greased 9-inch pans, spreading evenly with the back of the spoon. Bake three layers at a time on the middle rack at 350°F for 8 minutes. A layer is done when you hold it near your ear and don't hear it sizzle.
4. Let the layers cool a couple of minutes in the pans, then run a spatula around the edge and ease each out. Don't worry if one tears.
5. Spread a slightly cooled layer with cooled frosting, using two or three serving spoonfuls between layers, and stack to the 10th layer.
6. Cover the top and sides with the remaining icing, pushing any that runs onto the plate back onto the cake. You may need to wait for the icing to cool before icing the top and sides.

## Kitchen Notes

The recipe is from Mrs. Kitching's Smith Island Cookbook by Frances Kitching and Susan Stiles Dowell, published by Tidewater Publishers of Centreville, Maryland. NPR's page suggests optionally scattering crushed candy between layers, but not on the last.
